RunPod vs Vast.ai: Choosing a cloud GPU for AI image generation

Compare RunPod and Vast.ai for AI image generation: pricing models, setup, reliability, and how to stop cloud GPU charges when you are done.

If you want to rent a GPU for AI image generation, the practical answer is that RunPod and Vast.ai solve the same problem in different ways. RunPod sells fixed per-hour and per-second rates on named GPU types, with separate Pods, Serverless, and Clusters products. Vast.ai runs a marketplace where prices are set by supply and demand across 40+ data centers, with on-demand, interruptible, and reserved tiers. Neither is automatically cheaper for every workload: the right pick depends on whether you need the uptime commitments Vast.ai documents for its On-Demand tier, a specific GPU, or the lowest possible rate for a job that can be interrupted.

This comparison sticks to what each provider publishes on its own pricing pages, so you can map the numbers to your own image pipeline instead of guessing.

How RunPod prices GPU instances

RunPod splits pricing by workload type. RunPod GPU pricing lists Pods as dedicated GPU instances, Serverless as API-based inference billed by workers, and Clusters for multi-node jobs. The Pods table shows per-hour and per-second billing, and the page was updated September 27, 2026.

Representative Pod rates from that table include:

  • RTX A5000, 24 GB VRAM — $0.27/hr
  • A40, 48 GB VRAM — $0.49/hr
  • L4, 24 GB VRAM — $0.49/hr
  • RTX 3090, 24 GB VRAM — $0.50/hr
  • RTX 4090, 24 GB VRAM — $0.74/hr
  • RTX 5090, 32 GB VRAM — $0.99/hr
  • A100 PCIe or SXM, 80 GB VRAM — $1.59/hr
  • H100 PCIe, 80 GB VRAM — $2.89/hr
  • H100 SXM, 80 GB VRAM — $3.49/hr

RunPod also lists Serverless worker rates, which are a different billing model from Pods. For image work, the relevant Serverless entries include 24 GB 4090 PRO at $1.10/hr, 48 GB L40/L40S/6000 Ada/MIG 48GB PRO at $1.75/hr, and 80 GB H100 PRO at $4.79/hr. Storage is billed separately: container disk at $0.10/GB/mo, volume disk at $0.10/GB/mo running and $0.20/GB/mo idle, and network storage from $0.07/GB/mo under 1 TB down to $0.05/GB/mo over 1 TB.

RunPod also publishes public endpoints with per-request pricing for image models, including google / Nano Banana Edit at $0.0380 per request and google / Nano Banana Pro Edit at $0.14 per request. Those are API endpoints, not local installs — if you want to understand that distinction, see our guide on running AI image models without owning a GPU.

How Vast.ai prices GPU instances

Vast.ai GPU pricing is explicitly market-driven: "Prices are set by supply and demand across 40+ data centers." The page describes three instance types:

  • On-Demand — described as offering uptime commitments, per-second billing, and no interruptions, positioned as best for production.
  • Interruptible — "50%+ cheaper," preemptible, may be reclaimed, described as best for batch training and fault-tolerant workloads.
  • Reserved — up to 50% off with 1, 3, or 6 month terms, described as offering capacity guarantees, volume discounts.

Vast.ai states it supports 68+ GPU types from RTX 3060 to B200, with no long-term contracts required and no minimum hours. Because the platform is a marketplace, the live rate for a given GPU changes over time; the pricing page shows real-time cards rather than a fixed rate table, so any specific number you see today is a snapshot, not a commitment.

Cost comparison for image generation workloads

The honest comparison is not "which provider is cheaper" but "which billing model matches your job."

  • Short, interactive sessions. Both providers bill per second. RunPod publishes per-second Pod billing alongside per-hour rates; Vast.ai advertises per-second billing with no minimum hours and no rounding up. For a short image-generation test, the difference between the two is usually small relative to the GPU tier you pick.
  • Long batch runs that can restart. Vast.ai's Interruptible tier is the structurally cheaper option because it is preemptible and advertised as 50%+ cheaper. If your pipeline can checkpoint and resume, that discount is the main reason to choose Vast.ai.
  • Production endpoints. RunPod Serverless bills workers rather than a persistent instance, which fits request-driven image APIs. Vast.ai's On-Demand tier is positioned around its documented uptime commitments and no interruptions, which fits a persistent worker you control.
  • Storage. RunPod publishes explicit storage rates starting at $0.05/GB/mo. Vast.ai's pricing page does not list storage rates in the supplied text, so treat storage cost as unknown from that page and check the console.

One caveat: because Vast.ai prices float with supply and demand, a RunPod rate and a Vast.ai rate for the "same" GPU are not directly comparable unless you check both on the same day. RunPod's table is dated September 27, 2026; Vast.ai's is live.

Setup and reliability differences

RunPod's pricing page describes Pods as "dedicated GPU instances, deployed across 31 global regions" and notes thousands of GPUs across 30+ regions. That framing implies a managed, fixed catalog: you pick a GPU type, you get that GPU type. For image generation, that predictability matters when a workflow depends on a specific VRAM size — for example, a 24 GB card versus a 48 GB card changes which models and resolutions fit.

Vast.ai's framing is the opposite: a competitive marketplace where "more supply means lower prices." The trade-off is variance. Interruptible instances may be reclaimed, which the page explicitly labels as suitable for fault-tolerant workloads. On-Demand instances are described with documented uptime commitments and no interruptions.

Neither page makes a blanket reliability guarantee. RunPod mentions reserved clusters with "SLA-backed uptime" for enterprise contracts, and Vast.ai mentions documented capacity terms on Reserved instances. If you need an SLA, that is a sales conversation on both platforms, not a self-serve default.

Security considerations for cloud GPU work

Cloud GPU security is a real concern for image generation because you are often uploading reference images, LoRAs, or client assets to a machine you do not own. Neither pricing page in the supplied evidence describes encryption, data retention, or tenant isolation policies. That means you should not assume a specific security posture from the pricing pages alone — check each provider's compliance and privacy documentation before sending sensitive images.

For a broader discussion of what leaves your machine when you use a hosted image model, see our guide on cloud deployment for AI image models. The key distinction: a cloud GPU runs a model on rented hardware, while a hosted image API runs a model you do not control. A client library does not make a hosted model local or offline.

How to stop cloud GPU charges

This is where most beginners lose money. Both providers bill for time, and an idle instance still costs money.

  1. Stop the instance, do not just disconnect. Closing your SSH session or browser tab does not stop billing. Use the provider's stop or terminate control in the console.
  2. Terminate, do not stop, when you are done for the day. RunPod's storage table shows volume disk at $0.10/GB/mo while running and $0.20/GB/mo while idle — idle volume disk is billed at a higher rate than running volume disk. If you are not coming back soon, terminating removes the instance.
  3. Watch storage separately from compute. RunPod bills container disk, volume disk, and network storage independently of GPU time. A stopped Pod can still incur storage charges.
  4. Prefer per-second billing for short jobs. Both platforms advertise per-second billing, so a 15-minute render should not round up to an hour — but confirm the billing unit on the instance type you choose.
  5. Use interruptible or serverless for bursty work. Vast.ai's Interruptible tier and RunPod's Serverless workers both avoid paying for a persistent idle GPU, at the cost of preemption or cold starts respectively.

If you are still deciding whether you need a cloud GPU at all, our quickstart guide covers the baseline setup before you rent hardware.

Which should you pick?

Choose RunPod if you want a fixed catalog with published per-hour and per-second rates, a clear split between Pods, Serverless, and Clusters, and predictable GPU types for image workflows that depend on specific VRAM. Choose Vast.ai if you want market-driven pricing, are comfortable with variable rates, and can use Interruptible instances for batch image jobs that checkpoint and resume.

For most beginners running a first image generation experiment, the deciding factor is not the provider — it is whether you remember to stop the instance. Pick the cheaper tier for your job shape, set a reminder, and terminate when the render finishes.
